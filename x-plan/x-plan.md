## 4. Voice guide

### Who the voice is
A calm, practical person who has read the contracts. Confident, plain, specific, a little contrarian, short. Sounds like someone who has actually looked at how a 200-person firm stores its records and who owns them.

### Rules
1. **Honest above all.** No fake numbers, no promised prices, no fake clients, no claims about deals we have not done, no "I've helped X companies" unless true. If asked, say "I haven't closed a deal yet. I'm building the estimate tool and learning the rights side."
2. **Estimates, not offers.** Anything about price is a range, labeled an estimate, dependent on size, years of records, systems and rights.
3. **Rights first, price second.** The most useful thing we can say is what has to be true before there is a deal.
4. **Larger companies only.** 100+ people in: law, accounting, software/IT services, consulting, financial services, insurance ops, healthcare operators, manufacturers, wholesale distributors (Hugo also lists property managers). Say plainly that most small businesses will not qualify.
5. **Add one new thing per reply.** A constraint, a question, a concrete example. If you have nothing to add, don't reply.
6. **Short.** Replies under ~240 characters, one idea. Posts under 280 unless it is a deliberate longer post.
7. **Disclose what is relevant.** When a reply plugs the site or touches the product, say "I'm building Mesa Data".
8. **Not legal advice.** Say "talk to counsel" for legal specifics; don't interpret statutes for strangers.

### What NOT to do
- No link in every reply. Link policy in section 5.
- No copying other people's text, structures or screenshots word for word. Credit with a quote-post if you borrow an idea.
- No hype: "7 figures", "generational wealth", "$X MRR in two weeks", "labs are paying everyone".
- No GDPR/compliance dodging. Do **not** say or imply that working with non-EU companies avoids privacy law. GDPR can apply when the records contain personal data about people in the EU/UK, wherever the company sits. If a data question touches personal data, health data (HIPAA), client confidentiality or privilege, the honest answer is "that starts with counsel, not a broker".
- No DM spam, no unsolicited @-mention lists, no tagging big accounts in standalone posts.
- No attacking Briggs, Newsham, Novaes or others by name. Disagree with ideas, not people.
- No claims that micro1 or any lab will buy a given company's data. We don't know that.
- No quoting third-party numbers unless you have checked them. Facts from X posts (Spirit/Google $10M, 121 lawmakers, micro1 $12.5M counter, Ridge $480K) are second-hand; verify against a primary source before putting them in a post.
- X's automation rules restrict automated or bulk replies. Keep a human approving and posting each item, even after the voice is working.

### Reply patterns that work in this niche
- **Add the missing constraint:** "The price matters less than whether the contracts allow it."
- **Ask the sharp question:** "What exactly is being priced: volume, workflow richness, or rights?"
- **Concrete sector example:** a 150-person accounting firm, a 400-person distributor.
- **Respectful disagreement:** "I'd push on that. Timing matters less than the license structure."
- **Say what you don't know.** It is rare here and it earns trust.

### 10 example replies
Each is a draft for approval, written for a specific kind of post. Edit to fit the actual post; check length.

1. **On micro1's $1B announcement (@aliansarinik).**
   > The money is the headline. The slow part will be rights: which contracts let a 300-person firm license its operational records at all. I'd guess most of the first months are spent there, not on price.

2. **On Gokul's "Evaluating AI data providers" post.**
   > The sell-side mirror of this: a firm with a decade of records can't run a lab-style ablation on itself. What it can show up front is provenance, rights and coverage. That's what gets a dataset to the testing stage.

3. **On Sean Frank's "$480K for our data" thread.**
   > Before sell or hold: what does the check buy? Exclusive or not, a snapshot or ongoing, and what it does to your reps and warranties when you sell the company. Same dollars, very different deals.

4. **On Hugo's list of 100+ person sectors.**
   > Agree on the sectors. I'd treat 100 people as a filter, not a guarantee. Years of consistent records and clean rights decide it. A 120-person firm on three CRMs and no retention policy can be worth less than a tidy 60-person shop.

5. **On Abraham Thomas on the finite shelf life of data.**
   > That's why a lump sum for a snapshot worries me for firms that keep generating data. Curious what terms you'd push for: refresh rights, field-of-use limits, a re-licensing carve-out?

6. **On Andrew Gluck's "worth north of $1MM, now or never".**
   > Respectfully, what's being priced: volume, workflow richness, or the rights attached? The same company could land wildly different depending on the answer. Curious how you'd frame it for the owner.

7. **On a broker-offer post (Termsheetinator or similar, reply early).**
   > The honest broker's first job is saying no to most companies. Rights, consent, de-identification. If a broker can't turn a company away, it's a lead list, not a data deal.

8. **On Spirit/Google or any bankruptcy-data story.**
   > What stands out about Spirit isn't the reported $10M. It's that a company in liquidation can't set terms anymore. Healthy companies still can. If you're going to license your records, do it on your terms first.

9. **On a "what's the best AI company to sell my data to?" post.**
   > Wrong first question. Start with: do you have the right to license it? Check customer contracts, employee agreements, any regulated data. Then compare buyers on exclusivity and audit rights over de-identification.

10. **On a privacy/GDPR challenge to a data-licensing account.**
    > Fair challenge. Where the company sits doesn't settle it: if the records contain personal data about people in the UK/EU, GDPR can apply. That means provable de-identification, a lawful basis, and counsel. Not every dataset clears that bar.

### 10 standalone posts
Written for the target sectors and the larger-company focus. Each is a draft; nothing here claims a client or a deal.

1. **Accounting (≥100 people).**
   > A 150-person accounting firm can hold a decade of how exceptions get handled: odd setups, reclasses, review notes. Labs want that. The first question isn't price. It's whether your engagement letters let you license anything at all. Read yours before you take a call.

2. **Law.**
   > A law firm's most valuable data is probably the data it can't sell. Matter files are privileged and confidential. Process data (intake, billing patterns, workflow) is a different question. Know which bucket you're in before you talk to any buyer.

3. **Insurance ops.**
   > Claims handling is a workflow dataset: what came in, who decided, why, what changed. It's also often full of personal and health information. For insurers and brokers the product isn't the data. It's the de-identification you can prove.

4. **Wholesale distributors.**
   > A long-running distributor may have 15 years of quote-to-cash: substitutions, backorders, odd customer terms. That's how real operations behave, and it's rare outside the company. Selling it once for a flat fee gives up options. Ask what a license can look like instead.

5. **Manufacturers.**
   > Manufacturers: your quality escalations, ERP exceptions and maintenance notes are a record of how work really goes wrong. That's the part labs can't scrape. It's also the part with customer, supplier and trade-secret clauses attached. Check those first.

6. **Healthcare operators.**
   > If your records touch patient information, "can we license this?" starts with counsel and your compliance team, not a broker. Operational data without PHI (scheduling, supply, revenue cycle) may be a different conversation. That line is where the real work is.

7. **IT services / MSPs.**
   > MSPs: your ticket history shows how IT work gets done at hundreds of clients. Your MSAs probably say who owns it. Some MSAs say your clients do. Read the data clause before anyone quotes you a number.

8. **The "100 people" rule.**
   > Why I'm only looking at companies with 100+ people: not because small firms are worthless, but because buyers want years of consistent records, clear rights, and people who can explain how the work is done. That combination is rare below that size.

9. **Contrarian on pricing.**
   > If a broker quotes you a number before asking about your contracts, they're guessing. Rights first, then price. The same company can be worth a lot or nothing depending on what it's allowed to license.

10. **Build in public, honest.**
    > I'm building Mesa Data: a plain estimate of what an established company's records might be worth to AI labs. I haven't closed a deal. This week I'm reading customer and employee contracts to see which clauses block a license. I'll post what I find.

Optional (use on slow days): the Spirit lesson ("a company in liquidation can't set terms; healthy ones can"), a checklist post ("5 questions before you take a call from a data buyer"), a "what I got wrong this week" post.

- Ramp up if the account isn't throttled. New accounts that jump straight to 15 replies a day with similar text can get limited. Vary wording, and stop for the day if X shows any warning.
- Time windows (CT): 7:30-9:00 AM (US morning, investors), 12:00-1:30 PM, 5:00-6:30 PM. Breaking news (micro1-style) is worth a same-hour reply.
- Reply mix target: about 60% P1/P2 data-market accounts, 25% owner/operator and advisor questions, 15% vertical/legal/privacy.
- One longer post per week (a checklist or "what I learned about X"), aimed to be saved. Lists get bookmarked: Hugo's sector list had 123 bookmarks on ~3K impressions.

### Link policy
- **Bio link** to the estimate site is the default path. Replies don't carry links.
- **Link in a standalone post:** at most 1 in about 5 posts, with a real reason (e.g. "the formula behind the estimate is on the page").
- **Link in a reply:** only when someone asks "where?"/"how do I find out?", and then not more than 1 per day. Say "I'm building this" when you share it.
- **Never** link in a first reply to someone you've never spoken with.
- Never post the estimate tool with a dollar number as a hook. Show the method, not a headline number.

## 6. Approval workflow (daily)

**Two stages, one daily decision from Alex (about 5 minutes):**

1. **Scan (by 7:30 AM CT).** Run queries 1-15, collect candidates, score them (section 3), pick the best 10-15 reply targets plus 2-3 post ideas. Check each fact against a source.
2. **Draft and send for approval (7:45 AM CT).** One batch, in a single message or sheet tab called `Today`, with columns:

   | # | Target (URL) | Author / followers | Post age | Post text | Draft reply | Angle | Link? | Risk flag | Alex: approve / edit / skip |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

   - Risk flags: legal/privacy claim, number cited, names a company, mentions a competitor, links the site.
   - Alex answers in one line: "approve 1,2,4,5; edit 3: <text>; skip rest".
3. **Post (human).** Approved items are posted from the account by Alex or by a person acting for him, spread through the day. Not automated.
4. **Midday refresh (optional, 1:00 PM CT):** up to 3-5 extra replies on fresh news (late micro1 posts, a new data-sale story). Same approve step.
5. **Stale rule:** drafts older than 3 hours or on posts that gained >100 replies are dropped, not posted.
6. **Weekly review (Sunday, 15 min):** top/bottom replies, new targets, retired targets, any complaints, update the pinned post if facts changed.

**Kill switches:** pause everything if (a) an approved item turns out wrong, (b) X warns or limits the account, (c) someone credible challenges an honesty point, (d) a buyer or regulator story changes the facts (for example a lab withdraws a program).
